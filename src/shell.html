How it works

Your words become a spec: item, size, yarn weight, hook, gauge, stitch and features. Gauge sets the real size of one stitch (about 14 sc per 10 cm in worsted on a 5 mm hook); a different hook scales it, and a gauge you type overrides it. hdc is about 1.6 sc tall and dc about 2.3, which is how real fabric measures.

Hats are worked top-down in joined rounds. The target is the hat's true shape in centimetres: a flat crown circle (growing about 6 sts a round in sc, 8 in hdc, 12 in dc) out to the head circumference less the ease, then worked even, with FPdc/BPdc ribbing at the brim. A genetic search tries round-by-round stitch counts, scores them on size, flatness (no ruffles or cupping) and how easy the increases are, and keeps the best. Scarves, cowls and blankets search stitch and row counts, stripe repeats and granny-square grids that land on the size.

The winner is simulated at true size: hats stitch by stitch as a spring network that stretches over a head form; flat pieces as cloth draped over a rod, a box or shoulders. Yarn is estimated from the stitches (calibrated so an adult worsted beanie uses about 110–180 m) plus 10% for ends.

Walkthrough

The hook and yarn motions are a simplified teaching animation, not a physics-accurate yarn simulation. Close-ups play for every stitch at slow speeds, for the first of each kind at medium speeds, and are skipped at high speed so the piece just grows.
